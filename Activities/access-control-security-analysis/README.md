# 🔐 Access Control Security Incident Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project focuses on investigating an access control security incident involving an attempted unauthorized financial transaction.

A deposit was initiated from the business to an unknown bank account. The finance manager confirmed that the transaction was not authorized. Fortunately, the payment was stopped before completion.

As the cybersecurity professional responsible for investigating the incident, I reviewed the available access information, identified potential authorization weaknesses, and recommended security controls to reduce the likelihood of a similar incident occurring in the future.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The objectives of this investigation were to:

* Review access information related to the security incident
* Identify information that could help determine the potential threat actor
* Analyze weaknesses in the organization's authorization controls
* Identify unnecessary or excessive user access
* Recommend security improvements to prevent similar incidents

---

## 🔍 Incident Analysis

The investigation identified the following information:

* The incident occurred on **October 3, 2023**.
* The account involved was **Legal/Administrator**.
* The login originated from the IP address **152.207.255.255**.
* The account was associated with **Robert Taylor Jr.**, a former contractor.
* The contractor's agreement had ended in **2019**, but the account was still active and accessed business systems in **2023**.

These findings indicate a potential weakness in the organization's account management and authorization processes.

---

## ⚠️ Security Issues Identified

### 1. Inactive Contractor Account Remained Active

A former contractor's account remained active after the contractual relationship ended.

Accounts belonging to former employees or contractors should no longer have access to organizational systems. Failing to deactivate unused accounts can create opportunities for unauthorized access.

### 2. Excessive Access Permissions

The account had administrator-level access.

Administrative privileges provide significant access to organizational resources. Users should only receive permissions necessary to perform their assigned responsibilities.

---

## 🛡️ Security Recommendations

### Account Deprovisioning

Employee and contractor accounts should be disabled or removed when employment or contractual relationships end.

The organization should also conduct regular access reviews to identify accounts that are no longer required.

### Principle of Least Privilege

Users should receive only the minimum level of access necessary to perform their job responsibilities.

Administrative privileges should be restricted and regularly reviewed.

### Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Multi-factor authentication should be enabled to provide an additional layer of security.

MFA can help reduce the risk of unauthorized access when account credentials are compromised.

## 📊 Key Takeaway

This investigation demonstrates how weaknesses in access management can create significant security risks.

By implementing proper account deprovisioning, limiting user permissions through the principle of least privilege, conducting regular access reviews, and enabling multi-factor authentication, organizations can reduce the likelihood and potential impact of unauthorized access.
