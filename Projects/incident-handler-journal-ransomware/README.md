# Incident Handler's Journal – Ransomware Security Incident

## Project Overview

This project documents an incident-handler journal entry based on a simulated ransomware security incident involving a small U.S. health care clinic.

The scenario involves a targeted phishing campaign in which malicious email attachments allowed attackers to gain access to the organization's network. The attackers subsequently deployed ransomware that encrypted critical files and disrupted access to patient-related information and business systems.

## Incident Summary

**Incident Type:** Ransomware attack
**Initial Access:** Phishing email with malicious attachment
**Affected Organization:** Small U.S. health care clinic
**Reported Time:** Tuesday, approximately 9:00 a.m.
**Primary Impact:** Critical files and systems became inaccessible
**Attacker Demand:** Payment in exchange for a decryption key
**Cybersecurity Tools Used for This Journal Entry:** None

## 5 W's

### Who?

An organized group of unethical hackers targeted the health care clinic through phishing emails.

### What?

The attackers used a malicious attachment to install malware, gain access to the network, and deploy ransomware that encrypted critical files.

### When?

The incident was discovered on Tuesday at approximately 9:00 a.m., when employees reported that they could no longer access important files and software.

### Where?

The incident occurred within a small U.S. health care clinic and affected its computer systems and access to critical patient data.

### Why?

The attackers appear to have been financially motivated because the ransom note demanded money in exchange for the decryption key.

## Key Cybersecurity Lessons

* Phishing can provide an entry point for a larger security incident.
* Malicious attachments can introduce malware into an organization's environment.
* Ransomware can significantly disrupt business operations and access to critical information.
* Incident documentation is an important part of the incident response process.
* Security awareness and preventive controls can help reduce the risk of phishing-related attacks.
